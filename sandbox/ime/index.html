WHAT WE BUILD

From a single cell to a complete line.

Whether you need more throughput, relief from repetitive labor, better quality, or a line that finally runs as one system, we engineer automation around your product, your floor and the way you run. That can mean a single cell that solves one hard problem, or a complete multi-cell system designed, built and supported as one accountable project.

Production lines and packaging

Complete production lines

Sorting, case packing, cartoning, packaging, palletizing and connected process steps engineered as one continuous line — not separate automation islands.

Printing, labeling and gluing

Print, apply, verify and track labels, codes and variable data without adding a manual station. Automated gluing applies adhesive precisely and consistently, part after part.

Pallet stretch wrapping and handling

Automate stretch wrapping, pallet movement and outbound handling as part of the end-of-line system.

Vision, AI and custom tooling

Vision picking and sorting

2D and 3D vision systems identify, sort and pick product, including items that vary or arrive unpredictably.

AI vision inspection

AI vision systems inspect the product for dimensions, defects, presence or absence, orientation and quality. The cell classifies each part as pass, reject or rework, and records the result.

Custom tooling and mechanisms

Custom end-effectors, tool changers and mechanisms designed for your product when nothing off the shelf will do.

Robotics and material handling

Machine tending and material handling

Robots that load and unload machines and move product between stations through full shifts.

Autonomous mobile robots (AMRs)

Autonomous mobile robots that move material between cells and work areas — so multiple systems share flexible transport without fixed conveyors or operators pushing carts.

Robotic welding

Robotic welding cells for consistent, clean-finish welds that skip secondary filing — so parts move through production faster. The robot, fixturing, welding equipment and safety systems are engineered and tested as one system.

Software, data and safety

MES, ERP and traceability integration

Connect equipment and automation to MES, ERP, production databases, traceability systems and plant-level data.

Live-data and order-driven automation

Cells that connect to your order, inventory or schedule data and adjust on their own. Built-in process knowledge means new hires don’t have to rely on tribal knowledge.

Safety systems

Safety is engineered into every cell from day one. Collaborative robots may be deployed without perimeter guarding when the application-specific risk assessment supports it; high-speed cells are guarded as required. Each cell starts with a documented risk assessment and is designed to support compliance with applicable OSHA requirements.

Upgrades and support

Upgrades and retrofits

Revitalize underperforming or legacy equipment. We reprogram, optimize and upgrade existing robots, lines and controls so they run faster and more reliably, without starting over.

Maintenance and support

Ongoing maintenance, troubleshooting and engineering support for the systems we build, from the same team that designed and built them.

Robotic machine upkeep

Robots that take over the routine upkeep of your high-output equipment, such as consumable changes and cleaning, so there is less manual maintenance, less downtime and more of every shift spent producing.

    How it works

    1. Roll: the operator rolls the cart (housing your CRX and program) to a line and engages the Zero-Point Docking System, which locks it at a fixed point
    2. Dock: connect the retractable power and air lines; RFID sensors identify the station and signal the FANUC controller to load the right recipe
    3. Run: confirm the recipe on the teach pendant and press start; the cobot is ready to work with repeatable spatial alignment
    • Drop-in FANUC CRX compatibility: dedicated mounting footprints, interior space and ventilation for standard CRX controllers, plus a place for the factory teach pendant cradle on the cart
    • True lock and go repeatability (±0.035 in): the 4,000 lb toggle clamp anchors the cart in the same spot every time, so no vision alignment, point touch-up or recalibration after a move
    • Hardware-based safety interlocks: the robot cannot run unless the cart is locked into the docking plate. A turnkey safety foundation that ties into FANUC Dual Check Safety (DCS)
    • Smart station recognition: RFID-based station recognition simplifies PLC integration. A built-in coupling provides a mapped I/O handshake that can trigger the appropriate mode or recipe
    • All-in-one utility routing: auto-retracting power and air reels, integrated air regulator and condensate collector built into the chassis
    • Industrial mobility: dual-sided forklift slots for long moves and polyurethane wheels so one operator can position it easily

    Spiffy book feeder

    Spiffy

    Autonomous, turnkey book feeding for industrial print shops

    SEE SPIFFY IN ACTION Watch on YouTube ↗

    Maximize your post-press capacity. Spiffy takes three stacks of books and feeds them one at a time into the next step of your process. It was designed for 3-knife trimmers and also works with shrink wrappers, packaging lines and standard downstream setups. Built-in vision guidance, active anti-static hardware and dynamic mechanical calibration keep your finishing line running at capacity.

    3stacks fed one at a time
    ~375books per load*
    1 yr / 2,000 hrsfactory warranty†

    How it works

    1. Stage: operators quickly load stacks onto the 3-bank stack lifter; multi-stage queuing stages product on the fly, with no need to match downstream conveyor rates
    2. Scan and detect: starting the cycle positions each lifter bank at the output chute while a high-speed scanner reads the barcode to spot job changes and capture book thickness
    3. Dispense: feed straighteners catch and align the falling books for an orderly, uniform flow to your conveyor
    • Separator sorting and taper compensation: angled spacers help keep tapered, shingled stacks upright. During operation, the scanner detects the spacers and the chute pivots to divert them into a catch tray for reuse
    • On-the-fly speed optimization: reads each book’s thickness and adjusts timing for smooth, continuous flow
    • Networked production feedback: MQTT over TCP/IP sends real-time data to your Ethernet network and shop management systems, creating a digital paper trail of every item fed
    • Active static elimination: an ionizing anti-static fan neutralizes static for reliable feeding of plastics and glossy print
    • Industrial safety: photoelectric safety edges and high-visibility E-stops cut motor power when triggered. After the safety condition is cleared, an HMI reset is required before motion can resume
    • Two operating modes: continuous mode with a configurable batch pause so downstream lines can catch up, and manual mode for direct control during setup and testing
    • Utility and connectivity ports: external RJ45 port for controller access and remote diagnostics, a 120VAC 15A convenience socket, and an automatic HMI log of operational and safety events
    • Structural stability: heavy-duty steel frame built for demanding post-press work, minimizing vibration on your floor

    Factory warranty

    Every Spiffy is backed by a factory warranty covering 100% of certified repair labor and replacement parts for manufacturing defects, for up to one year or 2,000 operating hours, whichever comes first.† It is designed for high-capacity, heavy-duty industrial environments.

    Labor savings = operators affected × shifts × hours/shift × days/year × burdened rate, adjusted for uptime and the labor-value capture percentage, then grown with wage inflation. Benefits are scaled for the install lag (months until benefits start); year-one ramp applies to the first live year. Annual operating costs are deducted each year; support is charged from year two as a percent of cell cost. Optional residual value is added at the end of the period. Cost of delay is the net run-rate benefit forgone each month the project is postponed, plus the NPV lost by a 12-month deferral; it is context and is not part of NPV. Operating costs are charged for the full year from year one, and payback excludes residual value. Tax mode applies a straight-line depreciation shield on cell cost only — confirm treatment with your accountant. Strategic factors are qualitative and do not change the NPV.     The IME story

    Founder and President Leo Wright, a robotics engineer with twelve years at Reid Ashman Mfg., started IME Automation in April 2021 to build the automation partner he wished manufacturers had: engineers who stay with a project from first sketch to production, and solutions priced and sized to fit the job.

    It started with smaller companies that big integrators overlooked. Where others say impossible, IME digs in, reprogramming, prototyping and pushing limits, because nothing is impossible until we prove otherwise. Customers kept coming back for upgrades and more systems, and the work grew with them.

    As those customers grew, so did IME, from single cells to large robotic deployments and complete production lines. Some customers now operate multiple IME robotic cells. Today IME does it all: design, build, programming, testing, installation and support. The scale changed. The standards didn’t. The same engineers stay with the job from first sketch to production, and the same refusal to call anything impossible applies whether it’s one cell or a full line. And we haven’t forgotten our roots: every customer, whatever the size of the job, still gets an engineer who picks up the phone.
